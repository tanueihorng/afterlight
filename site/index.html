A living record of the sight you fought to keep.

Your vision changes every day. Your medical record usually does not. Afterlight keeps a continuous, day-by-day account of what you see — so nothing depends on what you can remember in a ten-minute appointment.

Open Afterlight

Free. No account. Nothing to install, though you can add it to your home screen. Works with no connection.

Why it exists

I had a retinal detachment. I had surgery at nineteen. My vision is still changing.

If you have been through that, you know the specific shape of the problem. The clinic sees you for fifteen minutes, twice a year. Between those visits you are the only person watching your eyes — and by the time you are back in the chair, the questions have collapsed into one sentence you do not trust: “I think it's been a bit worse?”

You forget which eye. You forget whether the new floater arrived before or after the last appointment. You cannot describe the shape of the shadow that was so clear at 2am. You are asked whether anything has changed and you genuinely do not know, because your record holds what the hospital did to you, not what you saw.

That gap is where the fear lives. Not in the diagnosis — in the not-knowing.

Everything else is what that habit makes possible six months later: a timeline you can actually read, drawings you can lay over each other to see drift, your scans and letters in one place, and — the part that earns the thirty seconds — a one-page brief for your next appointment saying what changed in each eye and what you wanted to ask.

Where your record lives

  You  →  Afterlight, in your browser
              ↕
       storage on this device

  optionally, if you choose:
       →  an export file you keep
       →  a printed page or a PDF

  no server · no account · no analytics · nothing uploaded

There is no backend. Records, drawings, scans and documents sit in your own browser's storage on your own device. Nothing is transmitted, because there is nowhere for it to be transmitted to. Not a policy that could change later — an architecture with no destination. A check runs on every commit that reads the built files and fails if anything would be fetched from anywhere.

The trade is honest and it is yours to manage. Clearing your browser data deletes your record, and nobody can restore it for you. Export regularly, and keep the file somewhere safe. It is your whole eye history; treat it like a medical document, because it is one.

What it refuses to do

  • It will not tell you what you have. The condition reference is something you navigate. It is never surfaced from your symptoms and never ranked against your record.
  • It will not score you. No risk scores, no probabilities, no red dashboards, no “your vision is declining”.
  • It will not judge a trend. It describes what you recorded — the dates, the values, the difference between the first and the last. Never better or worse.
  • It will not read your scans. It stores your OCT and shows it to you. It does not tell you what is in it.
  • It will not reassure you. A quiet week means you did not write anything down. It never means your eyes are fine.
  • It will not invent. Asking a question searches your own entries and cites them. There is no language model in it. When it cannot find something it says so.

Before you rely on it

The clinical wording has not been reviewed yet. The writing in Afterlight that describes eye conditions, explains the home checks, or tells you when to seek urgent care was drafted without clinical input and has not yet been reviewed by an ophthalmologist or optometrist. Treat every explanation as background reading rather than as advice about your eyes.

Your own record is accurate regardless — what you wrote, when you wrote it, and what your clinic gave you. That part is yours.

What is outstanding.

If something is sudden or severe, get seen first. Sudden floaters, sudden flashes, a curtain or shadow across your vision, or a sudden drop in vision are reasons to contact an ophthalmologist or an emergency eye service now. Write it down afterwards.

Afterlight is a personal record-keeping tool. It does not diagnose, does not interpret imaging, does not estimate risk, and does not replace an ophthalmologist.

Built for eyes that are having a hard day

  • Four themes, two of them high contrast, and text up to double size with the layout reflowing rather than clipping.
  • Every drawing and chart described in words, for a screen reader or for a day when the picture will not resolve.
  • Full keyboard use, large targets, and motion you can switch off.

If something here is wrong

If a screen is unusable, a word is wrong, or something would frighten someone at 2am, that is worth telling me: open an issue. Accessibility barriers and clinical accuracy concerns have their own forms, and both go to a person. That feedback is worth more than a feature request.

Please do not attach your own records to an issue. Describe the shape of the problem — it is almost always enough, and your record is yours.
