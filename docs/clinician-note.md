# If a patient hands you an Afterlight brief

One page, for the person on the other side of the desk.

---

## What this document is

A patient-generated summary of what one person recorded about their own eyes over a stated period.
It is produced by an app on their device from entries they made themselves, plus details they
copied from documents they hold.

It is **not** a clinical record, not a measurement, and not a diagnosis. Nothing in it has been
interpreted, scored, or judged by software. The app has no model in it: everything you are reading
was either typed by the patient or copied from a document by the patient.

## What it is for

To answer, with a record rather than a guess, the question the patient cannot usually answer:
_what has changed since last time?_

Between appointments, the patient is the only person observing their eyes. By the appointment,
that observation has usually collapsed into one unreliable sentence. This is an attempt to keep it
instead.

## How to read it in sixty seconds

The first page is the whole point.

| Where                       | What it is                                                                                                                                               |
| --------------------------- | -------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| **Top two panels**          | One per eye. Inside each, the patient's own reports come first, grouped as new / more than usual / unchanged / less than usual, newest first.            |
| **Second block in a panel** | Anything the patient copied out of a clinic document, headed separately. It is never mixed into the list above.                                          |
| **Questions**               | What they came to ask.                                                                                                                                   |
| **Below that**              | Context: their drawings, clinic events they hold records of, current treatment, and — only if they chose to add them — recorded numbers and home checks. |

Everything is dated. Where a date or an eye came from a filename or a scanned document rather than
from the patient typing it, the record is marked unconfirmed and is **excluded from this brief
entirely** until the patient has checked it.

## What the labels mean

- **Patient reported** — the patient's own description of their experience. Subjective, and
  presented as such.
- **Patient drawing** — a drawing they made on a visual-field canvas of what they perceive. It is
  a representation of experience, not a field test and not a retinal image.
- **Clinician documented** — copied by the patient from a letter or report you or a colleague
  wrote.
- **Device measurement** — copied from a device print-out or report.
- **Extracted from document** — read out of a file by the app, then confirmed by the patient. If it
  has not been confirmed, it is not here.

Severity numbers (`3/10`) are the patient's own rating of their own experience on their own scale.
They are comparable with that patient's other entries and with nothing else.

## What it deliberately does not contain

- No risk score, probability, or prognosis.
- No interpretation of imaging.
- No trend judged as better or worse. Where numbers appear, they are described arithmetically —
  counts, dates, values, the difference between first and last — and never characterised.
- No inference about which condition a symptom suggests.
- No claim that a quiet period means a healthy eye. A gap in the record means nothing was written
  down.

## Home checks

If the patient added the "checks I did at home" section, those are self-administered screen-based
checks under uncontrolled conditions — brightness, distance and correction are recorded because
without them the result is not comparable with anything, including the patient's own previous
attempt. They are not acuity measurements and are labelled as such throughout.

## Where the data lives

On the patient's own device, in their browser. There is no server, no account, and nothing is
uploaded. They can hand you a printed copy, a PDF, or an encrypted file covering a date range of
their choosing.

## If something in it is wrong

Tell the patient — they can correct any entry, and the record is theirs. If the _document itself_
is misleading, unclear, or worded in a way that would mislead a colleague, that is a defect worth
reporting: <https://github.com/tanueihorng/afterlight/issues>.

---

_Afterlight is a personal record-keeping tool. It does not diagnose, does not interpret imaging,
does not estimate risk, and does not replace clinical assessment._
