A guide for the person keeping the record. No jargon, no assumed familiarity with apps.

If you only read one page, read [keeping it safe](keeping-it-safe.md) — losing everything is the
one mistake that cannot be undone.

## Starting out

Open the site. There is nothing to sign up for, because there is nowhere to sign up to.

**Install it to your home screen.** On iPhone: Share → Add to Home Screen. On Android: the menu →
Install app. This makes it open like an app, work without a connection, and — importantly — makes
the browser far less likely to clear your record.

**Try the demo first if you want.** Settings → Load demo data fills the app with a made-up
patient's history so you can see what six months of entries looks like before trusting it with your
own. Everything demo is badged as demo and removable in one tap, and it never mixes with yours.

**Set your baseline early.** My Eyes → each eye → "what my vision is normally like". Write a few
sentences about your ordinary day: the floaters you always have, the blur you have got used to, how
it is in bright light. Everything afterwards is measured against this. Without it, "worse than
usual" has nothing to be worse than.

## The daily habit

This is the thirty seconds that makes everything else possible.

Open **Today**. You get one question and two answers.

**Nothing different today** — one tap, done. This is not an empty day. "I checked, and it was the
same" is a real observation, and six weeks of them is exactly what tells a clinic that a change is
new.

**Something changed** — then, and only then, a form appears:

- **Which eye.** Always. Right and left are separate stories and are never averaged.
- **What.** Floaters, flashes, a shadow, blur, glare, distortion, pain, and so on.
- **How it compares with your usual.** Same as usual, slightly more, much more, fewer, different in
  appearance, or new. This is the most useful field on the screen — more useful than severity —
  because it is the question you will be asked in the chair.
- **Severity, if you want.** Your own 0–10, about your own experience. It is comparable with your
  other entries and with nothing else.
- **In your own words.** "Like a hair drifting across the bottom left when I look at the sky." Write
  it the way you would say it. This is the part you will not be able to reconstruct in six weeks.

**When did this start?** If you noticed it last night and are writing it up this morning, tap
**Yesterday**, or pick another day. The entry is dated by when the change started, not by when you
typed it — otherwise your brief will say it began a day later than it did.

**Same as before?** Shortcuts built from things *you* have recorded before. Tapping one starts an
entry you can adjust; it saves nothing on its own. Afterlight never suggests a symptom you have
never mentioned.

**Gaps are fine.** There are no streaks, no reminders designed to make you feel bad, and no reward
for a perfect run. If you miss a fortnight, the app says gaps are fine, because they are.

## Drawing what you see

**What I See** gives you a canvas shaped like your field of view. Draw the floater, the shadow, the
distortion, roughly where you see it.

Tools for a dot, a strand, a ring, a blob, a shadow, a flash and a smudge of blur, each with size
and darkness. On a tablet with a pen, the app ignores your palm once the pen is down.

Two things worth knowing:

- **Overlay two dates** to see drift you cannot possibly hold in your head. A shadow that has grown
  over three months is obvious side by side and invisible from memory.
- **Every drawing is described in words too**, automatically — "a medium dark blob in the lower
  left, and two small dots near the centre". That description is what a screen reader reads, what
  goes in the appointment brief, and what is searchable.

Your drawings are your perception. They are not retinal images and are never presented as any.

## Your timeline

Everything, in order: your entries, your drawings, your appointments, diagnoses, procedures,
medication, scans and letters.

Filter by eye, by type, or by date range. Every row carries a badge saying where it came from — your
report, your drawing, a clinician's note, a device, or something copied out of a document. Those are
never blended.

## Each eye's own story

**My Eyes** is where the longer-lived things live, per eye:

- **Baseline** — what normal is for you, with a history of revisions when normal changes.
- **Diagnoses** — what has been documented, by whom, when.
- **Procedures** — surgery, laser, injections, with the operative note if you have it.
- **Floaters as objects** — the strand you have had for years, given a nickname, tracked as one
  thing over time rather than as fifty separate entries.
- **Measurements** — acuity, pressure, thickness, with the unit and the method they were taken by.
- **Prescriptions** — glasses, over time.

## Scans and letters

**Imaging & Documents** keeps the originals. They stay exactly as your clinic gave them to you.

**Adding a stack at once.** "Add files" takes a drop of several files. Afterlight reads what it can
from each — a date in the filename, an eye from `_OD`, a page count from a PDF, the study date and
laterality from a DICOM file — and shows every one as a field you check.

**It will not guess.** A filename with `04-09-2026` in it could be 4 September or 9 April, so it
offers both and waits for you. Everything read from a file is marked **Not checked yet**, and stays
out of your appointment brief, until you confirm it.

**Comparing OCTs.** Pick two dates and drag the slider between them. Your clinician's interpretation
takes precedence over anything you think you can see.

## Checks you can do at home

**Checks** has an Amsler grid, a distance vision check calibrated with a bank card held to the
screen, and a contrast check.

These are **comparisons with your own previous attempt**. They are not measurements of your vision
and cannot be compared with anything done at a clinic.

That is why you cannot save one without recording the distance, the lighting, the screen brightness
and whether you were wearing your correction. Two attempts done differently are reported as *not
comparable* rather than plotted together — which is the whole point of doing them.

## Preparing for an appointment

Add your appointment under **Appointments**, then tap **Prepare appointment brief**.

You get one page covering the period since your last visit:

- **What changed, per eye** — new, more than usual, unchanged, less than usual, newest first.
- **Your questions** — the running list you have been adding to between visits.
- **Your drawings**, your clinic events, and your current treatment.

Optionally, recorded numbers and home checks. They are off by default because one page is what gets
read.

**Set the period** to whatever the appointment is about — since the last visit, the last 7 or 30
days, or any two dates.

**Put your name on it, or do not.** There is a field for whatever you want at the top of every page:
a name, a date of birth, a hospital number, or nothing. Afterlight will not put your name on
anything you did not type there.

## Handing it over

Four ways, all of them local:

- **Save as PDF** — a proper document, laid out to be read in about a minute, with your drawings at
  print resolution. Choose A4 or US Letter.
- **Print** — a clean black-and-white page. Provenance badges print as words like
  `[patient reported]`, so nothing is lost on a mono printer.
- **Present fullscreen** — one section at a time in large, high-contrast type, paged with the arrow
  keys or a swipe, with the screen kept awake. For showing someone across a desk.
- **Share** — one encrypted file covering only the dates and record types you pick. It shows you
  exactly what is in it and what is not before it is made, and the default is narrow: your own
  entries and your questions, no scans, no clinic documents. You get a four-word passphrase to say
  out loud. Say it; do not send it with the file.

There is also a **code to show in the room** — a short summary someone can scan off your screen with
no signal at all. It is plain text and says so on its own face; anyone who photographs your screen
can read it.

If your clinician wants to know what they are holding, [clinician-note.md](clinician-note.md) is a
one-page explanation written for them, and the brief links to it.

## Searching and asking

**⌘K / Ctrl-K** searches everything. You can narrow it: `eye:left type:floaters after:2026-06`. It
knows clinical words and everyday ones both ways, so "OCT" finds "tomography" and the reverse, and
it forgives a typo in a long word.

**?** answers questions from your own history. *"When did glare in my left eye first appear?"*
*"How many times did I record flashes this year?"* *"What was my last pressure reading?"*

Every answer cites the entries it came from. There is no language model in it — it is a search over
your records with the arithmetic done for you. When it cannot find something, it says exactly that
rather than making something up.

## Making it readable

**Settings → Display**, and it is worth spending a minute here.

- **Four themes**, including two high-contrast ones — black on white and white on black.
- **Four text sizes**, up to double. The whole layout reflows; nothing gets cut off.
- **Reduced motion**, **glare comfort** (softens bright surfaces) and **dimmed imagery** (holds back
  bright scan images until you ask for them).

These are stored in your record, so they survive an export and come back on a new device.

Everything works by keyboard alone, and every image, drawing and chart has a text equivalent.

## Backup and moving device

Covered properly in **[keeping it safe](keeping-it-safe.md)**. The short version:

- **Settings → Export everything.** One file, everything in it.
- Keep two copies in two places. Test one.
- New device: install Afterlight, then Settings → Import a backup. Read the preview before it
  writes anything.
- Clearing your browser data deletes your record, and nobody can get it back for you.

## What the labels mean

| Badge | Meaning |
| --- | --- |
| **Patient reported** | You typed it. Your description of your experience. |
| **Patient drawing** | You drew it. A representation of what you perceive, not an image of your retina. |
| **Clinician documented** | Copied by you from a letter or report. |
| **Device measurement** | Copied from a device print-out or report. |
| **Extracted from document** | Read out of a file by the app. |
| **Not checked yet** | Read out of a file and not yet confirmed by you. It stays out of your appointment brief until you confirm it. |
| **Demo data** | Part of the sample record. Not yours. Removable in one tap. |
| **Not recorded** | Nothing was written down. It does not mean normal. |

## What it will never tell you

Set out in full in **[what it will not do](boundaries.md)**. In short: it will not tell you what you
have, will not score you, will not judge whether a trend is better or worse, will not read your
scans, and will not reassure you. A quiet record means you did not write anything down — never that
your eyes are fine.

**When something is sudden or severe, get seen first and write it down afterwards.** Sudden
floaters, sudden flashes, a curtain or shadow across your vision, or a sudden drop in vision are
reasons to contact an ophthalmologist or an emergency eye service now.

## If something is wrong

If a screen is unusable, a word is wrong, or something would frighten someone at 2am, that is worth
reporting: <https://github.com/tanueihorng/afterlight/issues>. Accessibility barriers and clinical
accuracy concerns have their own templates, and both go to a person.
